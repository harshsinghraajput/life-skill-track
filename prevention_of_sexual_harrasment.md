# Prevention of Sexual Harassment

## Q1. What kinds of behavior lead to sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment can take the form of verbal, visual, or physical conduct of a sexual nature. Unwelcome remarks about a person's appearance or attire, sexually suggestive jokes, crude language, and other unwanted sexual conduct can all constitute sexual harassment.

It generally falls into three categories:

### Verbal
- Making sexually suggestive jokes.
- Commenting on someone's body or clothing.
- Making sexual remarks or innuendos.
- Pressuring someone for sexual favors.
- Persistently asking someone on a date after being declined.
- Spreading rumors of a sexual nature.
- Using vulgar language.

### Visual
- Showing or circulating sexually explicit images.
- Displaying posters, sketches, or cartoons.
- Sharing inappropriate emails or text messages.
- Sharing other offensive material.

### Physical
- Unwelcome physical contact such as touching, hugging, kissing, or rubbing against someone.
- Obstructing a person's path.
- Making sexually suggestive gestures.
- Ogling or staring inappropriately.
- Sexual assault.

## Q2. What would you do if you experienced or witnessed such behavior, whether it happened once or repeatedly?

I would take the following steps:

- If I witnessed such behavior, I would stand by the person affected and report what I saw instead of overlooking it.
- If the conduct is severe, ongoing, or makes me feel unsafe, I would promptly reach out to the relevant authorities or the workplace's support mechanisms.
- I would keep a record of the incident, noting the date, time, location, individuals involved, any witnesses, and a description of what occurred.
- I would also hold on to any relevant emails, messages, or other supporting evidence.
